🧩 Assembly Workbench

Printed a multi-part kit? Drag the parts in, nudge each one roughly where it belongs, and Chromatory snaps the mating surfaces together for you. Then export the whole thing as one merged STL, ready for the slicer.

  • Load every part of a kit. No triangle limit, and none of it leaves your device.
  • Snap mating surfaces together automatically, or click two faces and let them mate
  • Organic sockets, not just flat cuts: the fit reads the joint's own outline and checks the spin all the way round
  • Close the joint gaps as you merge, so a kit prints as one solid instead of parts with a slot between them
  • Remove internal voids, and see them first in x-ray the way your slicer would
  • Scissor cuts through organic shapes: click a few points, the line hugs the sculpt's surface, and both sides preview before you commit
  • A square alignment peg and its socket, sized from the cut face, so the halves only go back together one way
  • Magnet pockets and drill-pin guides, bored to fit both sides of a joint
  • Route a channel for LED wire through a finished model, and carve it
  • Save one merged STL, or every part as its own print-ready file

💡 Light Studio

Drop in your STL and light it the way a photographer would. Preview NMM (non-metallic metal) and OSL (object-source lighting) with real cast shadows, so you can see where every highlight and shadow will land before you commit a drop of paint.

  • Silver and gold NMM, glowing OSL, matte through chrome. Each one is a single click.
  • Paint regions with a brush that stays on the surface and fills that stop at real edges
  • Painted areas can glow as light sources, and a dark source radiates shadow for void effects
  • Comic style, with an inked outline you can lock to the view you are painting from
  • A monochrome value study, for checking your lights before any colour goes down
  • Apply SVG decals as stencils or press them into the surface as indents
  • Print 3D airbrush masks and edge masks that shield one area while you spray the rest
  • Export a colour model to Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer, ready to print in filament
  • Assemble and paint in the same scene, and save it with its lights, look and camera

🔒 NMM without the guesswork

Non-metallic metal lives or dies by where the highlights sit, and real reflections move every time you turn the mini. So lock them. One click freezes the highlights exactly where they are, and you can orbit the whole model while they stay put. Every angle becomes a painting reference for the same NMM.

  • Silver and gold NMM looks that respond to your actual lights
  • Lock the highlights, then orbit. They stay right where you'll paint them.
  • Painted regions keep their own colours and finishes next to the metal
  • Capture a reference render from any angle you like

What's in the studio

Built around your paints

Chromatory knows what's actually in your paint drawer, so every match, mix and scheme it suggests is something you could sit down and paint tonight.

📷

Copy a mini from a photo

Point it at a painted miniature you love. Chromatory reads each area and works out a step-by-step scheme using the paints you already own.

🎨

Paint the model itself

Describe a scheme or match a reference, and it paints the regions of your actual 3D model. Every angle agrees, because there is only one model.

🎯

Colour match & mixing

Real colour science (CIEDE2000 and Kubelka-Munk) finds the closest paint you own, or the mix that gets you there.

🗄️

Paint inventory

Catalogue your collection with swatch photos, and name a paint straight from a snap of the bottle.

🌗

Shadow & highlight guide

Give it a scheme photo and a lighting render. For each element it hands back the shadow, midtone and highlight paints, and shows you where each one falls.

📓

A memory for every project

Photos, decisions, recipes and whole painting conversations land in the project log, so next month's session starts where this one ended.

🤖

AI painting coach

Advice, a painting log and scheme mockups, all built on the paints you really have. Runs on your own AI, a local model, or turns off completely.

🧩

Assemble and cut kits

Mate the parts of a multipart kit, close the joint gaps, remove the internal voids a slicer would print hollow, and split a model that is too big for the plate.

🖨️

Print what you painted

Export a colour model to Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer, or bake the cel shading into printable shades.

✂️

Masks, stencils and decals

Print 3D airbrush masks and edge masks that shield one area while you spray the rest, and apply SVG decals as stencils or press them in as indents.

🎬

Looks beyond realism

Comic style with an inked outline you can lock to your painting view, and a monochrome value study for checking the lights before any colour goes down.

🔒

Local-first and private

Everything runs on your own PC. Your data, photos and models stay with you. Pair your phone or browser to it whenever you want the bench in your hand.

Bring your own AI

Drive it from Claude or ChatGPT

Chromatory doesn't sell you another chatbot subscription. It plugs into the AI you already use. Connect Claude, ChatGPT, or a model running on your own PC, and your assistant gets Chromatory's actual tools: your real paint inventory, colour matching, your project history, and the Light Studio itself.

You, to your AI Here's a photo of a painted mini I love. Can I match this scheme with my paints?
Your AI, using Chromatory It reads the photo area by area, samples the real colours, and matches every one against the paints you actually own, with honest close-enough grades. No invented paints, no wishful substitutions.
You Paint my model like that so I can see it from the back too.
Your AI, using Chromatory It splits your STL into areas, colours each one, and renders the painted model from any angle. One consistent scheme all the way around, because it painted the 3D model, not four separate pictures.
You, next weekend Where did we leave off?
Your AI, using Chromatory Sessions are logged to the project, so the conversation picks up with the scheme, the mixes and the decisions already on the table.

Built on open standards (an MCP server and an OpenAPI tool server), so it isn't locked to one vendor: Claude Desktop and Claude Code, ChatGPT and Codex, LM Studio, Open WebUI and local models can all connect. Your AI account stays yours, and Chromatory's tools run on your machine.
